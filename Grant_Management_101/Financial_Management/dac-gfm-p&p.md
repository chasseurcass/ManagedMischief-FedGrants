**Introduction**

The Oklahoma District Attorneys Council’s primary functions are to strengthen the criminal

justice system in Oklahoma, to provide a professional organization for the education,

training and coordination of technical efforts of all state prosecutors, to maintain and improve prosecutor efficiency and effectiveness in enforcing the laws of this state, and to

provide financial, personnel, and other administrative duties to the Oklahoma District

Attorneys System.

The Council has seven divisions. These divisions are the Executive Division, Finance Division, Victim Services Division, Federal Grants Division, Training and

Outreach Division, the UVED Division, and the IT Division.

The Council’s financial transactions are accounted for using four independent systems.

The Oklahoma Office of State Finance is responsible for establishing the policies and

procedures for state financial transactions and for executing those transactions in

accordance with the various state statutes, federal regulations, and governmental

accounting and reporting standards.

The State Comptrollers office consists of the:

- Central Accounting Unit: Maintains the state central accounting system to ensure budgetary and financial compliance with laws and regulations through general ledger accounting and maintenance, payroll accounting, tax accounting and reporting activities, including processing of general ledger transactions for revenues and expenditures, cash allocations, centralized payroll tax and withholding payments, maintenance of employee earnings records, and issuance of year end tax forms, such as W-2 and 1099M.
- Performs audits and processes payments for the settlement of state expenditure claims and payrolls to ensure compliance with laws and regulations, custodian of expenditure documents and records, and maintains the state central vendor file.
- Prepares the State of Oklahoma comprehensive annual financial report as required by state law for use in analysis and decision making by state management, creditors and others; maintains reporting information for all agencies, funds, and component units included in the state reporting entity; and prepares the annual schedule of expenditures of federal awards.
- Agency Finance Office: Provides shared services for various state agencies via contract to streamline and provide more efficient and effective back office finance functions, including budgeting, accounts payable and reporting. OSF also provides shared services for procurement, human resources and payroll functions.

Within the District Attorneys Council three additional independent financial tracking

systems are utilized. The Finance Division, the Victim Services Division and the Federal

Grants Division maintain Access databases and web-based databases for tracking

1. various financial and grant related activities. These systems are referred to as Finance Tracker and Personnel Database (Finance Division), Victims Compensation Claim Form
2. Database, VOCA Grants Database, OSF Claims Database, and Awards/Collect Database (Victims Division), and the Grants Management System (Federal Grants Division),
3. OKGrants, and Grants Project Costing (GPC) Module within PeopleSoft.

This manual has been prepared to document the internal accounting procedures for the

Oklahoma District Attorneys Council (DAC). Its purpose is to ensure that assets are

safeguarded, that financial statements are in conformity with generally accepted

accounting principles, and that federal grant and matching funds are managed with

responsible stewardship. In addition the procedures will assist in:

1. Presenting and classifying projected historical costs of the grants as required for budgetary and evaluation purposes;

2. Providing cost and property control to ensure optimal use of funds;

3. Controlling funds and other resources to assure that the expenditure of funds and use of property conform to any general or special conditions that apply to federal grant funds;

4. Meeting the prescribed requirements for periodic financial reporting of operations; and 5. Providing financial data for planning, control, measurement, and evaluation of costs.

All personnel with a role in the management of the fiscal operations of the DAC are expected to adhere to the policies in this manual. It is the intention of the DAC that these policies and procedures assist in our commitment to present proper and accurate financial management and reporting.

**Cash Receipts Procedures**

The Receptionist receives all incoming mail. Each individual piece of mail is date stamped and forwarded, unopened, to the specific division that it is addressed to. Any mail that is not addressed to a specific division is opened, identified and forwarded to the appropriate division.

*Finance Division Procedures*

All checks received from the Receptionist are stamped with the date received and endorsed with a stamp by the Insurance Coordinator then forwarded to the Finance Clerk for the preparation of the appropriate deposit forms which are reviewed by the Accounting Manager before being submitted to the bank.

Next, the Finance Clerk deposits the funds to the appropriate account and bank within the State accounting system. The appropriate entries into both the agency’s Finance Tracker system and the State of Oklahoma’s accounting system are processed by the Finance Clerk. The validated deposit slip with the appropriate documentation and copies of the checks, are filed according to month received.

All checks are to be deposited within 24 hours of receipt. Any deposit not delivered to the bank should be locked in the Accounting Division’s locking safe. No deposit should be locked in the safe for more than 24 hours.

*Funds Received by Wire Transfer*

The Federal Grant Division Director, the Grants and Victims Financial Managers, or Director of Victims Services will request a wire transfer of funds. This request will be submitted through ASAP.

A person other than the one drawing the funds will enter the anticipated deposit information for the State Treasurer. When an e- mail is received from the State Treasurer’s Office that the funds have arrived, this person accepts the deposit with the invoices that were created during the initial draw process.

The Grants and Victims Financial Managers will monitor the transfer of funds and maintain the appropriate records for each transaction.

The Grants and Victims Financial Manager reconciles deposits to total cash received each month by comparing reports retrieved from the state’s PeopleSoft Accounting System.

**Cash Disbursements Procedures (Federal Grants Division)**

1. Incoming requests for funds from subgrantees (due by the 15th of each month) will be checked and approved into the appropriate grants management system by the Grants Programs Assistant. The system automatically generates an e-mail to the subgrantee informing them that their request has been received. Subgrantees with JAG, VAWA, SASP & RSAT grants will request reimbursement through the OKGrants System.

2. After all requests for funds have been entered, the File Clerk prints the draw summary report and verifies that the total agrees with the total of the individual request for funds.

3. The File Clerk forwards the draw request csv file to the Grants Financial Manager who prepares the data for upload into GPC and PS.

4. The Grants Financial Manager checks the draw request csv file to the PO balances, budget balances, and uploads the data to GPC to create the billing invoice for reimbursement from the federal customer.

5. When the funds have been received and posted to the appropriate cash account, the File Clerk enters requests for funds voucher information into State Accounting System for non-OKGrants subrecipients. The Grant Financial Manager creates the vouchers in PS for OKGrants subrecipients.

6. After vouchers are printed, approved by the authorized District Attorneys Council personnel and automatically budget checked by the State System, the file clerk prints and prepares the voucher packet.

7. The following morning, a designated employee (File Clerk) runs the reports to verify that the vouchers do not have errors and prints the batch slip and writes ‘SCANNED’ at the top of the first page of the batch slip. Once all the vouchers have been scanned, then a designated employee (File Clerk) faxes the batch slip to OMES; 521-3383. The fax confirmation and batch slip are stapled and filed. The folder is kept in the Finance Clerk’s office.

8. Once the checks are received from the State Treasurer’s Office, the File Clerk mails the checks to the subgrantees. Subgrantees are notified by email from the PeopleSoft system of direct deposit of funds.

9. The File Clerk then reprints one set of each voucher, enters payment information (voucher number, check number and date) in the Grants Division Grants Management System.

10. The File Clerk then files one voucher in the Finance Division voucher file and one, along with the request for funds (supporting documentation) in the subgrantee file.

11. Incoming requests for funds from DA subrecipients will be checked against PO balance, budget balance, and proper signatures. The Grant Financial Manager creates voucher for reimbursement in the PS system.

**Cash Disbursements Procedures (Victims Division)**

1. All disbursements made pursuant to the Crime Victims Compensation Act are processed in compliance with State law and the rules and regulations established by the Board. Disbursements to victims and their providers are made upon verification and approval of the three-member board or the Director of Victims Services. **Reconciliations**

*Cash Flow:*

The District Attorneys Council must monitor its cash balances in order to ensure that it does not have excess cash on hand.

*Bank Reconciliations*:

1. Monthly, the Grants and Victims Financial Manager prints the Summary of Receipts and Disbursements, deposit report and 6-Digit Object of Expenditure Detail Report from the State Accounting System.

2. When reconciling the cash accounts, the following items should be included in the procedures:

a. A comparison of dates and amounts of daily deposits as shown on the deposit report.

b. A comparison of receipts, disbursements and journal entries to be certain that both sides of the transactions have been recorded

c. A comparison of wire transfers dates received with dates sent.

3. Completed cash account reconciliations should be filed and available for review by the State Auditor’s Office, the Federal funding agency, the US Department of Justice Inspector General, the Comptroller General of the United States, or any of their authorized representatives.

4. The Grants and Victims Financial Managers upon completion of the cash reconciliations, advises the Finance Accounting Manager of adjustments for preparation of journal entries that need to be prepared.

5. The Finance Accounting Manager prepares and enters (into the State Accounting System) or submits the journal entries to the Office of State Finance for entry.

*Reconciliations of Other Accounts*:

1. Assets - These accounts will include cash and receivables.

a. Cash - The balances in cash accounts should agree with the balances shown on the cash account reconciliations for each month/quarter.

10

b. Receivables - The grant receivable should agree with the Office of Justice Programs, Office of the Chief Financial Officer’s Detailed Payment Reports and subrecipient receivable should agree with the balance of subrecipient advances less expenditures.

2. Liabilities - These accounts are described as accounts payable and encumbrances.

a. Accounts Payable - The balance in this account should equal the balance of vouchers which have been entered into the State Accounting System to be paid to vendors but checks have not yet been issued at the end of the accounting period.

b. Encumbrances – The balance in this account should equal the amount of unpaid purchase orders remaining payable to vendors and subgrantees.

3. Income/Expenses - Income accounts are described as income from Federal grantors and expenses are line items for salaries, personnel benefits, travel, contractual, supplies and operating, equipment, confidential funds and other.

a. Income - The amounts credited to the various cash accounts should be reconciled with drawdowns, detailed payment reports and subgrantee unexpended returned funds.

Expense Accounts:

b. Salary Accounts - The balances in the salary accounts should be reconciled with the amounts reported on the payroll distribution report.

c. Personnel Benefits - The balances in the salary accounts should be reconciled with the amounts reported on the payroll distribution report

d. Contractual - The amounts charged should be reconciled to the purchase orders.

**Conflicts of Interest Regarding Federal Award Funds**

Non-federal entities are required to use Federal funds in the best interest of the award program. Decisions related to these funds must be free of undisclosed personal or organizational conficts of interest, both in fact and in appearance. Non-federal entities are required to disclose in writing any potential conflict of interest to the

awarding agency or pass-through entity, as applicable. See 2 C.F.R. § 200.112

*Confict in Fact:*

In the use of grant award funds (direct or indirect), a recipient or subrecipient should not participate in any decisions, approval, disapproval, recommendations, investigation decisions, or any other

proceeding concerning any of the following people or groups:

•An immediate family member;

•

A partner; •An organization in which they are serving as an ofcer, director, trustee, partner, or employee;

•Any person or organization with whom they are negotiating or who has an arrangement concerning prospective employment, has a financial interest, or for other reasons can have less than an unbiased transaction with the recipient or subrecipient.

*Confict in Appearance:*

In the use of award funds, recipients and subrecipients should avoid any action which might result in, or create the appearance of:

•Using an ofcial position for private gain;

•Giving special treatment to any person;

•

Losing complete independence or objectivity; •Making an ofcial decision outside offcial channels; or

•Affecting negatively the confidence of the public in the integrity of the Government or the program

Typical confict-of-interest issues may include:

■ Less-than-arm’s-length transactions—the act of purchasing goods or services or hiring an individual from a related party such as a family member or a business associate of the recipient.

■ Not using fair and transparent processes for subrecipient decisions and vendor selection. These processes must be free of undue influence, and fair and transparent. Most procurement requires full and open competition.
