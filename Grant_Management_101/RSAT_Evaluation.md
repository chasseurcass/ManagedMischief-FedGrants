# **A. Program Basics**

A1. Program type clearly identified

- [ ]  Residential RSAT program
- [ ]  Jail-based RSAT program
- [ ]  Aftercare / community-based RSAT program

A2. Target population clearly identified

- [ ]  Adults
- [ ]  Juveniles
- [ ]  Mixed (adults + juveniles)

If mixed, note whether services and data are clearly separated by population.

- [ ]  Yes
- [ ]  No

A3. RSAT funds clearly tied to a distinct program

- [ ]  Program has a name or track (not just “services”)
- [ ]  Eligibility criteria for RSAT participants are described
- [ ]  RSAT participants are tracked separately from the general population

# **B. Substance Use Treatment Component**

This is the “T” in RSAT. There must be a real, defined SUD treatment component.

B1. Evidence-based / evidence-informed SUD treatment

At least one is clearly identified:

- [ ]  Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for SUD
- [ ]  Motivational Interviewing focused on SUD
- [ ]  Moral Reconation Therapy (MRT)
- [ ]  Relapse prevention model / curriculum
- [ ]  Other named SUD treatment model (described in narrative)
- [ ]  Medication-assisted treatment (MAT) available or coordinated, where appropriate

If none of the above is clearly identifiable, flag for follow-up.

B2. Qualified provider

- [ ]  A licensed or credentialed provider delivers SUD treatment (or is contracted)
- [ ]  Role and qualifications are described
- [ ]  Treatment frequency / dosage is described (e.g., weekly groups, individual sessions)

B3. Substance use monitoring and response

- [ ]  The program conducts UA / other drug testing for participants
- [ ]  The application describes how positive tests are handled (clinical response, not just punishment)
- [ ]  There is a process for updating the treatment plan based on test results

# **C. Behavioral / Criminogenic Change Component**

This is where CBT for crime thinking, family interventions, etc. fit. Supports RSAT goals beyond just “stop using.”

C1. Behavioral interventions identified

At least one is described:

- [ ]  Cognitive-behavioral groups for thinking/behavior
- [ ]  Family-based model (e.g., Functional Family Therapy, MST, BSFT)
- [ ]  Trauma-focused therapy
- [ ]  Skills groups (coping skills, anger management, decision making)

C2. Behavioral work tied to substance use recovery

- [ ]  Application explains how behavioral work connects to SUD treatment goals (not just “general counseling”)
- [ ]  Treatment or case plans include both SUD goals and behavior/criminogenic goals

# **D. Aftercare / Continuity of Care**

RSAT requires planning for what happens after the residential / jail phase.

D1. Reentry / aftercare planning

- [ ]  Reentry planning starts before discharge/transition
- [ ]  Each participant has an aftercare plan documented
- [ ]  Plan includes continued SUD treatment in the community (or facility step-down)

D2. Community linkages

- [ ]  Identified community SUD providers
- [ ]  Identified MH providers, if needed
- [ ]  Coordination with probation/parole or juvenile supervision, if applicable

# **E. Stability & Wraparound (General)**

For adults, “stability” usually means housing, income, pro-social supports.

For juveniles, see the separate Section H as well.

E1. Housing / placement stability (adults)

- [ ]  Program assesses housing status before release
- [ ]  Plan includes stable housing or shelter placement at exit
- [ ]  Program coordinates with housing resources or reentry housing where available

E2. Employment / meaningful activity (adults)

- [ ]  Program connects participants to employment, training, or other structured daytime activity
- [ ]  Workforce/education supports are described (either RSAT-funded or leveraged)

E3. Pro-social support

- [ ]  Program links participants to pro-social supports (peer groups, recovery groups, mentoring, etc.)
- [ ]  Plan describes how these supports are engaged after release

# **F. RSAT-Funded Case Management / Coordination**

Case management should support the treatment and stability components. This is written as a simple decision tree.

F1. Is RSAT funding paying for case management / care coordination staff?

- [ ]  Yes
- [ ]  No

If No, skip to Section G.

If Yes, continue.

F2. Is there at least one clearly defined SUD treatment service in the application (Section B)?

- [ ]  Yes
- [ ]  No → Flag. Case management alone is not enough.

F3. Is there at least one clearly defined behavioral/criminogenic intervention (Section C)?

- [ ]  Yes
- [ ]  No → Note: treatment may be SUD-only; consider whether this is acceptable for your state, but flag for discussion.

F4. Is there a stability / reentry component identified (Section E and/or H)?

- [ ]  Yes
- [ ]  No → Flag for follow-up (case management has nothing concrete to stabilize).

F5. Case management functions (check all that apply)

- [ ]  Coordinates SUD treatment appointments
- [ ]  Tracks attendance and follows up on missed sessions
- [ ]  Communicates with clinicians about participant progress/relapse risk
- [ ]  Addresses barriers to attending treatment (transportation, scheduling, etc.)
- [ ]  Helps implement the reentry/aftercare plan
- [ ]  Documents contacts and actions

F6. Budget concentration

- [ ]  RSAT case management / coordination costs are a minority of the RSAT budget
- [ ]  RSAT case management / coordination costs are roughly half of the RSAT budget
- [ ]  RSAT case management / coordination costs are the majority of the RSAT budget

If case management is the majority of RSAT cost AND Sections B (SUD treatment) and H (juvenile stability) are weak or unclear, flag for deeper review.

# **G. Outcomes and Performance Measures**

These should reflect RSAT’s purpose: reducing substance use, relapse, and recidivism, and improving reentry.

G1. SUD-related outcomes

- [ ]  Change in substance use (self-report, UAs, or other measures)
- [ ]  Completion of SUD treatment episodes
- [ ]  Linkage to community SUD care post-release

G2. Behavioral / justice outcomes

- [ ]  New arrests or technical violations tracked
- [ ]  Recidivism / return to custody tracked over a defined period

G3. Reentry / stability outcomes

For adults:

- [ ]  Housing status at exit and follow-up
- [ ]  Employment / training status at exit and follow-up

For juveniles: see Section H.

# **H. Additional Checks for Juvenile RSAT Programs**

Use this section in addition to Sections B–G whenever the target population includes juveniles.

**H1. Juvenile-specific SUD treatment**

- [ ]  SUD programming is appropriate for adolescents (age-appropriate content, adolescent-specific approaches)
- [ ]  Screening and assessment tools for SUD are appropriate for youth
- [ ]  Youth are linked to ongoing adolescent SUD treatment after release, not just “referred somewhere”

**H2. Placement / living-situation stability (juveniles)**

For each participant, the program should have a plan for where the youth will live and how that supports recovery.

- [ ]  Application describes how the program assesses whether the home environment is safe and suitable
- [ ]  If the home is not suitable, there is a process to work with child-welfare / courts on alternative placement (kinship, foster, group home, etc.)
- [ ]  If group home or foster care is used, the program describes how it coordinates with that placement to support SUD treatment and behavior goals
- [ ]  The plan explains how placement decisions connect to relapse-prevention and supervision plans

You can flag for follow-up if:

- The only plan is “the youth returns home,” with no discussion of safety or suitability.
- There is no mention of how RSAT staff coordinate with child-welfare or court stakeholders when the home is unsafe.

**H3. School / education stability (juveniles)**

- [ ]  The program addresses school re-entry or continuation (enrollment plan, school contact, or similar)
- [ ]  There is a plan to coordinate with school staff (counselors, alternative school, etc.) where needed
- [ ]  Attendance or school engagement is part of what the program monitors or supports

**H4. Mental health and trauma supports (juveniles)**

- [ ]  Application recognizes that many youth will have co-occurring MH or trauma issues
- [ ]  Youth can be connected to MH services (counseling, psychiatry, trauma-informed care)
- [ ]  MH services are coordinated with SUD treatment and not in conflict with it

**H5. Family / caregiver engagement**

- [ ]  If family is safe/appropriate, the program has a plan to engage caregivers in treatment and reentry
- [ ]  If family is unsafe or not available, the program identifies alternative supportive adults (kin, foster caregiver, mentor, etc.)

**H6. Juvenile outcomes**

In addition to SUD and justice outcomes in Section G, juvenile RSAT programs should track:

- [ ]  School enrollment/attendance outcomes
- [ ]  Placement stability outcomes (e.g., remained in same placement for X months)
- [ ]  Connection to ongoing SUD and MH services after release

# **References (for you to check later)**

**RSAT statute and core program documents**

- 34 U.S.C. 10421 et seq. – Residential Substance Abuse Treatment for State Prisoners (RSAT) statutory authority.
- BJA “Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) Program” overview and program goals (Bureau of Justice Assistance).
- BJA / OJP RSAT Program Fact Sheet and Program Brief (goals: reduce demand for drugs, provide treatment, support aftercare).
- RSAT Program FAQs (types of programs, expectations for residential/jail-based/aftercare services).

**RSAT practices and aftercare / case management**

- NIJ “Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) Study: Characteristics and Components of RSAT Funded Treatment and Aftercare Services” (role of case management and aftercare requirements).
- “Promising Practices Guidelines for Residential Substance Abuse Treatment” (examples of effective RSAT program designs and components).

**Juvenile SUD treatment and stability**

- SAMHSA Advisory: “Screening and Treatment of Substance Use Disorders among Adolescents” (based on TIP 31 and TIP 32) – guidance on adolescent-specific SUD screening and treatment.
- SAMHSA TIP 32: “Treatment of Adolescents With Substance Use Disorders” – details on adolescent-appropriate SUD treatment approaches.
- OJJDP / related juvenile justice resources on reentry and behavioral interventions (e.g., model programs for MST, FFT, BSFT and the importance of education and placement stability in reentry).
