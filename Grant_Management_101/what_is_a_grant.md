A Comprehensive Guide to the Office of Justice Programs (OJP) Grant Lifecycle

1.0 Introduction: Understanding OJP Funding Instruments

This guide provides a clear, step-by-step overview of the Office of Justice Programs (OJP) grant process. Its purpose is to ensure that both applicants and grant managers understand their distinct roles and responsibilities at every stage of the grant lifecycle, from the initial application through the final closeout. By outlining each phase of this collaborative effort, this document aims to foster a transparent and efficient funding process for all stakeholders.

Defining Federal Funding

The Office of Justice Programs utilizes three primary legal instruments to distribute federal funds. Each instrument is defined by the level of government involvement required and the specific purpose of the funding.

Grant A grant is a legal instrument which permits the Office of Justice Programs (OJP) to: (1) transfer money or anything of value to an eligible recipient to accomplish a public purpose of support as authorized by Federal statute; and (2) requires no substantial involvement between OJP and the recipient during the performance of the activity.

Cooperative Agreement Similar to a grant, a cooperative agreement is a legal instrument that permits OJP to transfer money or something of value to a recipient to accomplish a public purpose. However, unlike a grant, a cooperative agreement requires substantial involvement between OJP and the recipient during the performance of the contemplated activity.

Contract A contract is a legal document used to acquire goods, construction, or services for the Federal government's direct benefit or use.

Dissecting Grant Types

OJP awards two distinct types of grants, each determined by how funds are allocated and awarded.

Discretionary Grants These grants are awarded directly by OJP to eligible recipients at the discretion of the agency. Key characteristics include:

* They are most often awarded on a competitive basis, requiring applicants to submit proposals in response to solicitations.
* Applicants must find and apply for these competitive grant solicitations on Grants.gov.
* In some cases, discretionary grants may be awarded on a non-competitive basis, often as a result of congressional direction.

Formula or Block Grants These grants are awarded to eligible recipients based on a statutorily created formula. The process for these grants is defined by law rather than agency discretion. Key characteristics include:

* The allocation of funds among eligible recipients is specified by statute or appropriations acts.
* The award amount is calculated by a formula that may consider factors such as population, census data, juvenile offender population, or crime statistics reported to the FBI.
* Examples include the OJJDP Juvenile Accountability Block Grants Program and the OVC VOCA Victim Compensation Formula Grants.

Understanding these fundamental funding types is the first step for any organization before engaging in the OJP grant application process.

2.0 The Grant Lifecycle Phase 1: Application and Submission

The application phase is of strategic importance in the grant lifecycle. This initial stage is critical, as meticulous preparation and strict adherence to procedural requirements by the applicant are foundational for a successful review. Both the applicant and the OJP grant manager have distinct responsibilities to ensure the application is complete and ready for evaluation.

Applicant Responsibilities

Applicants must take several precise steps to ensure their application is successfully submitted and processed.

1. Registration and System Submission: The application process involves two separate systems. Applicants must first register in and submit the SF-424 and the SF-LLL through Grants.gov. Following this, they must complete and submit the full application, including all attachments, in JustGrants.
2. Registration Timeline: Advance preparation is crucial. The validation and confirmation process for registration can take 5-7 business days, so applicants must complete this well before any application deadlines.
3. Solicitation Review: Applicants must thoroughly review the solicitation package to ensure all required components are complete. This includes understanding all reporting requirements and determining how the organization will collect and report the necessary data.
4. Information Provision: Throughout the review process, applicants have an ongoing responsibility to provide any additional information requested by the grant manager.
5. Application Tracking: Applicants can follow their application's progress online via the "Track My Application" feature on Grants.gov or directly within the JustGrants system.

Grant Manager Responsibilities

Simultaneously, the OJP grant manager performs key actions to process the incoming application.

* Application Generation: The grant manager generates the application within the system to initiate the formal review process.
* Completeness Check: The manager conducts an initial review to ensure the application is complete and that all requirements outlined in the solicitation have been addressed.
* Information Requests and Process Management: If any information is missing or unclear, the manager is responsible for requesting the necessary additional details from the applicant. The grant manager orchestrates the entire ongoing review process, which includes the programmatic, peer, and financial reviews detailed in the next phase.

Once a complete application is successfully submitted and verified, it enters a rigorous, multi-stage review process.

3.0 The Grant Lifecycle Phase 2: The Application Review Process

The application review phase is a structured evaluation conducted by OJP to ensure that funded projects are meritorious, fiscally sound, and aligned with programmatic goals. This comprehensive process involves several distinct steps to assess every aspect of the application.

1. Application Review This initial step serves as a basic screening. The application is reviewed to confirm registration information, check for completeness, and ensure that the applicant meets all of the basic eligibility requirements as defined in the solicitation.
2. Programmatic Review The assigned grant manager reviews the application to ensure the information presented is reasonable and understandable. This review assesses whether the activities proposed are measurable, achievable, and consistent with the program or legislative requirements stated in the solicitation. The results of programmatic review are provided to grant decision makers, who use that review along with other relevant factors to assess applications, and make funding decisions.
  * Peer Review: For competitive discretionary applications, a peer review is required. This process utilizes non-federal subject matter experts to provide an objective, independent evaluation of the application's merits, strengths, and weaknesses.
3. Financial Review The Office of the Chief Financial Officer (OCFO) conducts a financial review of all discretionary awards and cooperative agreements. This critical assessment evaluates the applicant's fiscal integrity and financial capability. The OCFO also examines proposed costs to determine if they are reasonable, necessary, and allowable under applicable Federal cost principles and agency regulations.
4. Award Decisions OJP adheres to a general timeline for notifying applicants. Grant awards are typically announced no later than September 30 of the calendar year. Unsuccessful applicants are generally issued a rejection letter by December 30 of the same calendar year.
5. Final Approved Award The final award decisions must be thoroughly documented to ensure transparency and accountability.
  * For awards that underwent a peer review, the recommendation memorandum must include a list of all applications received (from the lowest-scoring funded application to all higher-scoring ones) and a brief explanation for any higher-scoring application that was not funded.
  * For discretionary awards made without a peer review, the documentation must clearly explain the choices made, the reasons for those choices, and the policy considerations on which the decisions were based. An otherwise uninformed reader should be able to understand the process used and the final decisions made.
  * If a final decision-maker alters a recommendation, the change must be documented. This documentation must specify who made the decision to vary from the recommendation and provide the reasons for doing so.

Once a final award decision is made and documented, the process transitions from application review to active grant management.

4.0 The Grant Lifecycle Phase 3: Post-Award Grant Management

Receiving an award marks the beginning of a formal partnership between the grantee and OJP. This post-award phase requires active management, diligent monitoring, and consistent reporting from both parties to ensure the project's success and maintain compliance with federal guidelines.

Grantee Responsibilities

Upon receiving an award, the grantee must undertake several key administrative and managerial duties.

1. Award Acceptance: The grantee must access the award package in JustGrants, designate a Financial Point of Contact, and return the signed award document to the Office of the Chief Financial Officer (OCFO) within 45 calendar days of the award date.
2. Compliance Review: It is the grantee's responsibility to carefully review all special conditions attached to the award document and determine how the organization will comply with them.
3. Project Management: The grantee must manage the project in accordance with the grant terms and conditions, the OJP Financial Guide, and any special conditions included in the award.
4. Financial Reporting: Grantees are required to submit quarterly Financial Status Reports as stipulated in the OJP Financial Guide.
5. Programmatic Reporting: Grantees must submit Programmatic Progress Reports according to the frequency established in the special conditions of the award document.
6. Award Modifications: If changes to the project are necessary, the grantee must request approval for any award modifications as defined in the OJP Financial Guide.
7. Deliverables: The grantee is responsible for completing all deliverables outlined in the original application, the solicitation, or any special conditions of the award.

Grant Manager Responsibilities

During the post-award phase, the OJP grant manager provides crucial oversight and support.

* Initial Review and Contact: The manager reviews the grant terms, objectives, and conditions and contacts the grantee to discuss the requirements of the grant.
* Monitoring: The manager must monitor grantee compliance with programmatic, administrative, and fiscal requirements of relevant statutes, regulations, policies, guidelines, and with grantee stated objectives, and implementation plan. This is accomplished through desk reviews and/or site visits.
* Data and Reporting: The manager ensures that the grantee is reporting required data and is responsible for reviewing and approving the grantee's Progress Reports.
* Modifications and Oversight: The manager approves any necessary modifications to awards and conducts on-site monitoring visits to review grant activities.
* Cooperative Agreements: For cooperative agreements, which require substantial OJP involvement, grant managers provide additional direction and oversight to the grantee.

Successful grant management throughout the performance period culminates in a formal closeout process.

5.0 The Grant Lifecycle Phase 4: Grant Closeout

The closeout is the final stage of the grant lifecycle. This formal procedure ensures that all programmatic work has been completed, all federal funds have been properly accounted for, and all administrative requirements have been met by both the grantee and OJP.

Grantee Closeout Responsibilities

Within 120 days after the official end date of the grant, the grantee must complete all closeout activities and submit all required documentation, including:

* A Final Programmatic Progress Report
* A Final Financial Report
* Confirmation of compliance with all special conditions
* A complete Financial Reconciliation
* Submission of all required deliverables

OJP Closeout Responsibilities

OJP follows a specific internal timeline to finalize and officially close the grant.

* Program Office: The OJP Program Office must review the grantee's submission and forward the complete closeout package to the Office of the Chief Financial Officer (OCFO) within 120 days after the grant's end date.
* Office of the Chief Financial Officer (OCFO): The OCFO is responsible for officially closing the grant within 180 days after the end of the grant.

The OJP grant process is a structured, collaborative effort from start to finish. It relies on clear communication and a thorough understanding of the distinct responsibilities held by applicants, grantees, and OJP program managers throughout the entire grant lifecycle to ensure the effective and accountable use of public funds.
