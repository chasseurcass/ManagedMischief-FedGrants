BRIEF

Why Functional Family Therapy, Case Management, and Service Coordination Cannot Be Counted as RSAT Substance Use Disorder Treatment Without Licensure

Federal RSAT funds are restricted to substance use disorder treatment and treatment-linked aftercare delivered by licensed or certified professionals. This requirement comes from statute and program guidance. Under these rules, supportive services cannot be reclassified as SUD treatment unless they meet clinical criteria and are delivered under appropriate licensure.

The three primary services OJA funds under RSAT—functional family therapy (FFT), case management, and service coordination—do not meet those criteria.

1. Functional Family Therapy (FFT)

What FFT is:
FFT is an evidence-based, family-centered behavioral intervention that improves family functioning, reduces recidivism, and strengthens youth–family relationships.

Why FFT is not SUD treatment:

FFT is not a clinical substance use disorder treatment modality unless it is formally incorporated into a treatment plan created by a licensed clinician.

FFT does not diagnose, assess, or treat substance use disorders.

It does not require SUD certification to deliver.

Providers delivering FFT must be trained in FFT, but they are not required to hold SUD treatment licensure through ODMHSAS.

Therefore, unless FFT is delivered under the supervision of a certified SUD treatment provider and documented as part of a SUD treatment plan, it is a supportive service, not RSAT-qualifying treatment.

2. Case Management

What case management is:
Case management links youth to services, schedules appointments, monitors progress, and provides general support.

Why case management is not SUD treatment:

Case management is an administrative and supportive function, not a clinical service.

Case managers do not diagnose, assess, or treat SUD.

RSAT allows case management only when it supports treatment, not when it replaces treatment.

If RSAT funds case management in the absence of licensed SUD treatment, those activities fall squarely into OJJDP’s scope.

3. Service Coordination

What coordination is:
Coordination involves arranging referrals, communicating with families, and connecting youth to community resources.

Why coordination is not SUD treatment:

Coordination links youth to services; it does not provide treatment.

Coordination is not a clinical intervention and requires no SUD licensure.

RSAT coordination must link directly to ongoing SUD treatment, not general support.

If RSAT-funded coordination does not involve certified SUD providers, it cannot be counted as RSAT treatment or aftercare.

4. Licensure Requirements

Under RSAT:

Treatment must be delivered by licensed or certified SUD providers.

Aftercare must continue clinically supervised treatment.

Providers must appear on the state SUD provider registry or be practicing under an agency that is certified.

Non-clinical nonprofits like PIVOT may deliver supportive services, but without licensure or certification, they cannot fulfill the statutory definition of RSAT treatment.

5. Structural Conclusion (framed neutrally)

When RSAT activities consist solely of FFT, case management, and service coordination with no certified SUD treatment provider involved, the RSAT program is not implementing the treatment-focused elements required under the grant. The funded services align entirely with OJJDP’s scope rather than RSAT’s core statutory purpose.

This is why supportive services cannot be counted as RSAT treatment without licensure, certification, and clinical integration.

# 1. Legal Reference Sheet: 34 U.S.C. 10424

### Direct citations tied to RSAT treatment requirements

Below is the statutory text broken into requirements. Only the relevant subsections are included, and each item corresponds to a checkbox from your supervisor’s RSAT vs. supportive services checklist.

---

## A. RSAT must provide actual “substance abuse treatment”

**34 U.S.C. 10424(b)(1)**
Funds may be used for programs that “provide residential substance abuse treatment in correctional facilities.”

Meaning:
If the service is not **substance abuse treatment**, it does not qualify.

---

## B. Treatment must be delivered in a dedicated residential therapeutic unit

**34 U.S.C. 10424(b)(2)**
Programs must “engage participants for a period of time sufficient to address the individual’s needs, in a residential facility or dedicated treatment unit set apart from the general population.”

Meaning:
Treatment must be:

* Structured
* Residential or in a separate treatment unit
* Not general case management, family support, or mentoring

---

## C. Treatment must be based on **evidence-based practices**

**34 U.S.C. 10424(b)(1) and (b)(3)** refer to “substance abuse treatment and related services,” which the program guidance interprets as including **evidence-based** clinical SUD interventions.

Meaning:
CBT-SUD, MET, MI, MAT, etc. qualify.
FFT, case management, and generic therapy do not meet the definition unless clinically supervised and integrated into a SUD treatment plan.

---

## D. Aftercare must be **treatment-focused**

**34 U.S.C. 10424(b)(3)**
Programs must “ensure that individuals who participate in the program are provided with aftercare services.”

Federal guidance clarifies that aftercare must:

* Continue SUD treatment after release, or
* Connect individuals to a certified SUD provider

Meaning:
Case management alone does not satisfy aftercare requirements.

---

## E. RSAT is not allowed to fund wraparound as the sole program

Although RSAT allows “related services,” the statute’s only required activities are:

* Residential substance abuse treatment
* Treatment-linked aftercare

Supportive services are **supplementary**, not the core of the program.

Meaning:
If a state uses 100 percent of RSAT funds for case management, FFT, mentoring, or coordination, it violates the statutory structure even if wraparound services are helpful.

RSAT exists to fund **substance use disorder treatment** and **treatment-linked aftercare**.

# 1. What legally counts as a “clinical substance abuse provider” under RSAT

The RSAT statute **does not** define the provider type in detail, but it points to state law. That means the controlling standard is:

### A provider must be:

1. **Licensed or certified under state SUD treatment standards** (for Oklahoma, ODMHSAS).
2. **Authorized to deliver SUD treatment services** (not merely supportive services).
3. Delivering services that meet the statutory definition of **“residential substance abuse treatment”** (34 U.S.C. 10424(b)(1)–(3)).

In Oklahoma, a provider qualifies only if:

* The **agency** is certified by ODMHSAS as a substance abuse treatment facility, OR
* The **individual clinicians** working under that agency hold SUD treatment credentials, AND
* Those clinicians deliver SUD treatment services as part of the program.

**No certification = not a clinical provider.
No licensed clinicians = not a clinical provider.
No treatment services = not a clinical provider.**

This is not interpretation. It’s black-letter compliance logic.
