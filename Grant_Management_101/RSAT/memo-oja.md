MEMORANDUM: Analysis of J-RAP Funding Alignment with RSAT Statutory Requirements

1.0 Purpose of Analysis

The objective of this memorandum is to provide a detailed compliance analysis of the Oklahoma Office of Juvenile Affairs' (OJA) Juvenile Relapse Avoidance Project (J-RAP). This analysis will evaluate the project's alignment with the statutory requirements of its federal funding source, the Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) for State Prisoners Program, as administered by the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA).

This analysis will critically evaluate J-RAP's operational activities, program design, and funding allocation, demonstrating a systemic inversion where federal funds support administrative overhead and reentry services rather than the direct clinical treatment mandated by the statute.

To provide a comprehensive context for J-RAP's current structure, this memo will further contrast the project's model with the stated objectives of two other federal grant programs: the Second Chance Act (SCA) Youth Reentry Program and the OJJDP Title II Formula Grants.

2.0 Statutory Requirements of the Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) Program

A precise understanding of the RSAT program's legal framework is essential for an accurate compliance review. This section defines the core federal requirements that serve as the official benchmark for this analysis, based on the BJA FY24 RSAT solicitation. These mandates outline the specific clinical focus and structural components that all funded programs must implement.

Core Mission and Clinical Focus

Per the statute, the primary purpose of the RSAT program is to support the provision of Substance Use Disorder (SUD) treatment to individuals during detention or incarceration. The program is designed to create and implement residential SUD treatment programs within state prisons and local correctional facilities where individuals are confined for a period sufficient to permit effective treatment. As a key clinical priority, BJA encourages the provision of Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT), which includes the use of FDA-approved medications in combination with counseling and behavioral therapies, as part of any evidence-based substance use disorder treatment program.

Mandatory Program Structure for Juvenile Facilities

The RSAT statute (34 U.S.C. 10421 et. seq.) establishes the following non-negotiable structural requirements for residential programs in juvenile facilities:

* Duration The program must engage individuals with SUD for a period of 6 to 12 months.
* Therapeutic Community Participants must be "set apart from the general correctional population" in a dedicated housing unit or facility, establishing a therapeutic community environment.
* Drug Testing The program is required to implement periodic and random drug testing for participants, both while in the program and during community supervision post-release.
* Aftercare The program must provide aftercare services for participants upon their release from the facility.

Aftercare Services Requirement

The RSAT statute mandates that states must ensure individuals who participate in the residential treatment program are provided with aftercare services upon release. This requirement emphasizes coordination between the correctional treatment program and social service and rehabilitation programs, including education, job training, parole supervision, and recovery housing. The goal is to create a "warm handoff" from the in-facility program to community-based providers to ensure continuity of care and support successful reintegration.

These statutory requirements provide the framework against which the Oklahoma OJA's J-RAP program will now be evaluated.

3.0 Analysis of J-RAP's Operational Compliance with RSAT Mandates

This section critically assesses the J-RAP program's design and operational reality against the specific RSAT statutory mandates detailed in the previous section. This direct comparison is strategically important for identifying areas of alignment and significant divergence, which is crucial for determining overall compliance.

J-RAP Program Design and Funding Allocation

The stated purpose of the J-RAP program is to serve juveniles exiting Level E group homes and secure facilities who are at high and moderate risk of relapse upon their return to the community. An analysis of the program's funding structure reveals a clear separation between the use of federal RSAT funds and state funds.

Federal RSAT funds are explicitly allocated for program coordination and reentry support services, not direct clinical treatment. Funded items include:

* Personnel: An "OJA JRAP Aftercare Coordinator," "Workforce Development Specialists," and a "Career Navigator/Liaison."
* Support Services: Concrete supports designed to remove reentry barriers, such as assistance with application fees, housing, and restitution repayment.

In contrast, the actual clinical substance abuse treatment delivered by licensed providers is funded by "leveraging our existing state-funded Community at Risk Services (CARs) funding and JRAP state funds with the RSAT funds." This structure creates a "funding inversion," where federal grant funds intended for clinical treatment are instead allocated to administrative and reentry support functions, while state funds cover the primary therapeutic mandate.

Divergence from RSAT Clinical Priorities

A key BJA priority for RSAT funding is the expansion of Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) to address the overdose crisis. The BJA encourages applicants to support expanding the use of and access to FDA-approved medications in prisons and jails. However, an analysis of J-RAP's billable services reveals an exclusive focus on psychosocial and behavioral interventions, such as assessments, individual and group counseling, and rehabilitative treatment. The program's service array makes no provision for medical or pharmacological interventions like MAT, further distancing its clinical reality from the funding agency's stated priorities.

Evaluation of Structural Non-Compliance

A direct comparison between J-RAP's operations and the core structural mandates of the RSAT program reveals two key areas of non-compliance. This failure to meet mandatory program requirements constitutes a fundamental structural misalignment with the RSAT statute. The following table provides a visual summary of this assessment.

RSAT Mandate	J-RAP Operational Reality & Analysis
Program Duration (6-12 Months)	Non-Compliant. The J-RAP narrative explicitly states, "Most youth will complete their group home stay in less than six months," failing to meet the minimum statutory duration required for residential programs in juvenile facilities.
Therapeutic Community ("Set Apart")	Non-Compliant. RSAT requires a therapeutic community "set apart from the general correctional population" within a correctional or detention facility. The OJA's RFA for Level E group homes defines them as "community-based" settings. As such, they are statutorily distinct from the required correctional environment.
Drug Testing	Compliant. J-RAP problem statements from multiple years confirm that "all youth are subject to random testing via a 12-panel" or "13-panel urinalysis," thereby meeting this requirement.

This structural non-compliance is not accidental; it is the logical outcome of a program designed around an entirely different federal grant framework, as the following section will demonstrate.

4.0 J-RAP's Conceptual Alignment with OJJDP Reentry and Prevention Frameworks

The operational misalignments identified above are clarified when the J-RAP program is viewed through the lens of other federal juvenile justice programs. J-RAP's design aligns perfectly with the Office of Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention's (OJJDP) strategic priority of building a "community-based continuum of care" for prevention and reentry, a framework fundamentally different from BJA's RSAT mandate for treatment during incarceration. This section demonstrates that J-RAP's design, origins, and core activities align almost perfectly with OJJDP's Second Chance Act and Title II Formula Grant objectives.

Alignment with Second Chance Act (SCA) Reentry Objectives

The primary goal of the Second Chance Act is to support the successful reintegration of individuals returning to the community from incarceration. It specifically authorizes funding for "educational, literacy, and vocational services and transitional job strategies." J-RAP's core activities, funded by the RSAT grant, mirror these objectives directly.

* Workforce Development: J-RAP's use of RSAT funds to hire "Workforce Development Specialists" and "Career Navigators" to connect youth with employment and career pathways is a central objective of SCA-funded reentry programs.
* Program Origin: The J-RAP narrative provides a critical piece of context, explicitly stating that the project's design originated from a "Georgetown Capstone Juvenile Justice Reentry Initiative." This confirms the program was conceived as a reentry model, historically associated with SCA-style efforts, rather than a residential treatment program.

Alignment with OJJDP Title II Delinquency Prevention Framework

The OJJDP Title II Formula Grant program supports states in operating "prevention, diversion, treatment, and rehabilitation programs" aimed at improving the juvenile justice system. The J-RAP narrative contains a structural element that is statutorily unique to Title II grants.

The program documentation offers youth the option to participate in a "sub-committee of the State Advisory Group (SAG) on Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention." The establishment and maintenance of a State Advisory Group (SAG) is a mandatory requirement for states receiving Title II grants but is not mentioned in the RSAT statute. The inclusion of the SAG in the J-RAP model is a definitive indicator that the program is conceptually anchored in the system-improvement and delinquency-prevention framework of Title II.

Reliance on Non-RSAT Intervention Models (Functional Family Therapy)

The J-RAP narrative heavily features Functional Family Therapy (FFT) as a primary clinical intervention for youth returning to the community. However, this model is not funded by RSAT, and its purpose further illustrates the project's conceptual leanings.

FFT is described as a "proven in-home model" designed to treat delinquent youth in their community and reduce out-of-home placement. OJA's expansion of this program is supported by a $1.5 million grant from the Arnall Community Funds at Oklahoma City Community Foundation, a source entirely separate from the federal RSAT award. Leveraging a community-based prevention model like FFT, which is designed to keep youth out of facilities, further demonstrates that J-RAP's conceptual framework is rooted in preventing deeper system involvement (a Title II goal) rather than providing substance abuse treatment during incarceration (the core RSAT mandate).

This strong conceptual and structural overlap with OJJDP-administered programs explains the operational divergence from RSAT's mandates.

5.0 Conclusion: A Reentry Program Funded by a Treatment Grant

This analysis concludes that the Oklahoma Office of Juvenile Affairs' Juvenile Relapse Avoidance Project (J-RAP), while addressing critical needs for justice-involved youth, operates as a reentry and delinquency prevention program conceptually aligned with the Second Chance Act and OJJDP Title II Formula Grants. It does not operate as a residential substance abuse treatment program as defined and mandated by the RSAT statute that funds it.

This conclusion is supported by several key findings that demonstrate a fundamental misalignment between the program's funding source and its operational reality:

* Misapplication of Funds: Federal RSAT funds are allocated to administrative and reentry coordination staff (e.g., Workforce Specialists, Aftercare Coordinators), while the actual clinical substance abuse treatment is covered by state funds and other resources.
* Failure to Meet Statutory Structure: The program fails to meet the mandatory 6-to-12-month duration and the "set apart" therapeutic community requirements of the RSAT statute, as its target population resides in community-based group homes for less than six months.
* Alignment with Alternate Federal Programs: The project's design is explicitly based on a reentry initiative (Georgetown Capstone) and incorporates key structural elements of an OJJDP Title II program (the State Advisory Group), demonstrating its strategic purpose is aligned with system improvement and post-release support rather than residential treatment.
