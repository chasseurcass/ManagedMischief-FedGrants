## 1. How to adjust the RSAT application for new MAT programs

Add the following sections to the application: 

* who they will treat
* how the workflow will look
* how to estimate meds and staffing
* how RSAT fits with their other payers


### A. MAT Population & Volume Worksheet

1. Average daily jail population
2. Estimated percent with opioid use disorder (OUD)
3. Of those, percent likely to accept MAT
4. Typical length of stay for people with OUD
5. Expected average MAT census per month (you can even give them the formula)

Example prompt:

* “In the last 12 months, how many adults booked into your facility had clear indicators of OUD (self-report, history, prior MOUD, overdose, etc.)?”
* “Of those, how many would you reasonably expect to start or continue buprenorphine if it were available?”
* “Based on length-of-stay, what is your estimated average number of people on MAT per day?”

### B. MAT Clinical Model Description

* Will you use buprenorphine only (preferred), or also naltrexone
* New inductions vs continuity of care only
* Who will prescribe (MD / DO / APRN / PA) and how often they are onsite
* How you will handle:

  * screening and confirmation of OUD
  * induction protocol
  * dose adjustments
  * continuation of community prescriptions
  * release planning and community linkage

> “Describe, step by step, how a person with OUD will be identified, started or continued on buprenorphine, monitored, and linked to care on release.”

### C. MAT Budget Worksheet (you hand them the structure)

Give them a simple table to fill in instead of expecting them to guess what to include.

**Example RSAT MAT Budget Table**

* Personnel

  * Prescriber FTE and cost
  * RN / LPN time for induction and monitoring
  * Counselor or SUD clinician
  * Case manager / peer support
* Medications

  * Buprenorphine–naloxone (generic)
  * Any other MOUDs if applicable (you can tell them “methadone not allowable under this grant if you want to exclude it”)
* Labs / Testing

  * UDS
  * Basic labs if required by local protocol
* Implementation

  * Staff training on MAT
  * Policy/procedure development time
  * EHR or paper form changes
* Security and pharmacy

  * Lockboxes, storage, dispensing systems if needed

### D. Sustainability and Payer Mix

Ask:

* “How will MAT services be funded after RSAT support ends or diminishes?”
* “Will you bill Medicaid or other payers once the inmate is released?”
* “Will any in-facility MAT costs be picked up by county or state funds after startup?”

## 2. Reasonable cost benchmarks for buprenorphine MAT


### A. Medication cost benchmarks

Recent sources suggest:

* Generic buprenorphine–naloxone 8 mg / 2 mg films or tablets

* Retail price often around $130 for a typical 30-day supply, but real-world out-of-pocket with discounts can be roughly $30–$130 per month for many patients ([GoodRx][1])
* Other reviews and treatment centers often quote generic buprenorphine in the ballpark of about $100 per month in medication cost for maintenance doses ([Rise Above Treatment][2])
* Suboxone brand films can be much higher (up to $240–$300+ per month) and you can simply say in your guidance that generic is expected for RSAT budgeting. ([Recovery Delivered][3])


> “For planning purposes, assume generic buprenorphine–naloxone costs between $100 and $300 per person per month depending on dose and local contract pricing. Please obtain a local quote from your pharmacy vendor and plug that into the budget worksheet.”

Example:

* Assume 20 people on MAT per month
* Assume $150 per patient per month medication cost (midpoint)
* Meds = 20 × 150 × 12 = $36,000 per year

They can adjust the number of patients and local price.

### B. Program (non-medication) costs

One budget impact analysis for buprenorphine treatment in an outpatient setting found annual costs for visits and counseling around $100k–$150k for a moderate-sized caseload, excluding societal cost offsets. ([PMC][4])

For jail RSAT MAT, I would not hand them a single “average per patient” number, because staffing and scale vary. Instead, you can give them a structure and a rough per-FTE guidance like:

* Prescriber

  * 0.1 to 0.3 FTE physician / APRN may be enough for a small jail MAT program
* Nursing

  * 0.2 to 0.5 FTE RN (depending on census and induction volume)
* Counselor / SUD specialist

  * 0.5 FTE or more if you expect to deliver structured counseling groups as part of RSAT
* Case manager / reentry

  * 0.25 to 0.5 FTE


* Use their local salary scales
* Multiply by FTE
* Add fringe and an admin/overhead percentage


> “Show how you calculated each personnel line item (FTE × annual salary × benefits).”

### C. Overall per-patient cost anchor


* Medications: 100–300 per patient per month
* Clinical care (visits, counseling, labs, nursing): a few hundred per patient per month, depending on staffing and volume

Many MOUD cost summaries suggest that, in practice, all-in MOUD treatment often falls in the general range of several hundred dollars per patient per month, and is cost-effective compared with no treatment when you account for reduced hospital and justice costs. ([UNC School of Medicine][5])

## 3. What it looks like to implement MAT for an organization that has never done it


### Step 1. Planning and governance

* Form a MAT steering group

  * Jail administrator
  * Medical director or contract prescriber
  * Nursing lead
  * Behavioral health lead
  * Security representative
  * Reentry / probation rep
* Review state OUD/MAT guidance and RSAT expectations
* Decide:

  * which meds (here, buprenorphine / possibly naltrexone)
  * whether you will do new inductions, continuity only, or both

### Step 2. Policy and protocol development

* Screening and assessment

  * How every new admission is screened for OUD
  * How you will confirm diagnosis and current prescriptions
* Induction protocol

  * Criteria for starting buprenorphine
  * Withdrawal assessment
  * Dosing schedule and maximums
* Maintenance and monitoring

  * Visit schedule
  * UDS schedule
  * How you document cravings, side effects, and dose changes
* Release planning

  * How many days’ supply, or prescription plus linkage to community provider
  * Warm handoffs to local OTPs / office-based providers

You can ask applicants to attach or outline their draft protocols as part of the RSAT app.

### Step 3. Staffing and training

* Identify prescriber(s) who will write buprenorphine
* Train custody and nursing staff on:

  * what buprenorphine is
  * diversion concerns
  * basic overdose risk
* Train clinical staff on:

  * OUD screening
  * how to integrate counseling with MAT
  * relapse planning in a correctional context

### Step 4. Pharmacy and diversion control

* Decide how meds will be obtained

  * In-house stock via contract pharmacy
  * Local 340B partner, if relevant
* Storage and chain of custody
* Directly observed administration procedures
* Waste documentation, incident reporting, and diversion response

“Describe how you will obtain, store, and administer buprenorphine while minimizing diversion.”

### Step 5. Integration with RSAT counseling

The MAT toolkits emphasize that medications plus counseling and support is the standard. ([National Sheriffs' Association][7])

* “Describe how counseling or groups will be coordinated with MAT, particularly for people with OUD receiving buprenorphine.”

# RSAT MAT Quick Start Guide for New Applicants
A step-by-step guide for jails and correctional facilities seeking RSAT funding to launch a Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) program focused on buprenorphine.

---

## 1. Understand What RSAT Will Fund
RSAT funds **substance use disorder (SUD) treatment** delivered in secure settings.  
MAT is allowable when it includes:
- Screening for OUD
- Diagnosis and clinical assessment
- Buprenorphine induction and maintenance
- Counseling that specifically addresses SUD
- Release planning and warm handoffs

RSAT **will not** fund general mental health counseling, FFT, or unrelated services unless tied to SUD treatment.

---

## 2. Decide Your MAT Model
Choose the medications and workflow you will implement.

Most applicants use:
- **Buprenorphine–naloxone (preferred)**
- New inductions **and/or** continuation of community MOUD

Avoid methadone unless contracted with an OTP.

Define clearly:
- Who screens
- Who diagnoses
- Who prescribes
- How induction works
- How patients are monitored
- How you manage release and community linkage

---

## 3. Estimate Your MAT Population
Use last year’s booking and medical data to estimate:
- How many individuals present with OUD
- How many are likely to accept/continue MAT
- How long they stay
- Your average monthly MAT census

These estimates drive your medication budget and staffing needs.

---

## 4. Build Staffing and Training Capacity
You will need:
- A qualified prescriber (MD, DO, APRN, or PA)
- Nursing support for induction and monitoring
- A counselor who can deliver SUD-specific programming
- A case manager for release planning
- Security staff trained on diversion prevention

Train all staff involved before launching.

---

## 5. Design Secure Medication Procedures
Plan for:
- Procurement (local pharmacy, contract, or 340B partner)
- Secure storage and logging
- Directly observed dosing
- Diversion control (mouth checks, documentation, incident reports)
- Daily inventory reconciliation

---

## 6. Estimate Your Budget
Typical expense categories:
- Personnel (prescriber, nursing, counseling, case management)
- Medication costs (estimate $100–$300 per patient per month for buprenorphine)
- Labs and drug testing
- Training and implementation costs
- Storage/security materials

RSAT can fund startup and early implementation, but sustainability planning is required.

---

## 7. Prepare for Release Continuity
Your program must show:
- Partnerships with community MAT providers
- Appointment scheduling before release
- Plan for medication or prescription at release
- Process for warm handoffs

Better continuity = stronger application.

---

## 8. Complete the RSAT MAT Application Forms
The following forms must be submitted:
1. MAT Population & Volume Estimation Form  
2. MAT Clinical Model Description Form  
3. MAT Budget Worksheet  
4. Pharmacy & Diversion Control Form  
5. Staffing & Training Plan  
6. Release & Continuity-of-Care Plan  

Complete all sections clearly and justify assumptions.

---

## 9. Submit Supporting Attachments (If Applicable)
Recommended attachments:
- Draft MAT protocols
- Draft induction procedure
- Staff training plan
- Letters of collaboration with community providers

---

## 10. Contact the Grant Office for Technical Assistance
If you are unsure about:
- Medication estimates  
- Staffing levels  
- Budget assumptions  
- Compliance requirements  

Reach out early. Assistance is available throughout the application window.
