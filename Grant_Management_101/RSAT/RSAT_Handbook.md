Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) Program: A Performance Measurement and Reporting Handbook
Introduction: Purpose and Scope of this Guide
This handbook establishes the operational framework for Residential Substance Abuse Treatment (RSAT) program managers to ensure consistent and accurate data collection and reporting. It synthesizes the performance measures established by the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) into a clear, actionable guide. Diligent reporting is not merely a grant requirement; it is essential for demonstrating program effectiveness, ensuring accountability to stakeholders, and contributing to the national understanding of what works in substance abuse treatment for justice-involved populations. Adherence to these standards allows BJA to assess the collective impact of RSAT programs nationwide and to provide targeted support to its grantees.
It is important to note that the performance measures outlined in this document reflect the version REVISED SEPTEMBER 2025. Furthermore, program managers should be aware of the following official disclaimer: "The performance measure questions presented here are preliminary and may be subject to revision. This document is only to be used for planning and data collection purposes."

1.0 General Grant Administration and Operational Status
Reporting on general grant administration provides the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) with a critical, high-level snapshot of your grant's lifecycle stage and operational activity. These initial metrics are fundamental for federal oversight, allowing BJA to monitor progress, identify potential barriers, and offer timely support to grantees.
1.1 Reporting Period Status
During each reporting period, you must indicate whether it is the final period for which your award will have data to report. Answering "Yes" indicates that your program is concluding its grant-funded activities. For example, you should answer "Yes" if all funds have been expended and the award is in the process of closing out in the Grants Management System. This response also serves as the trigger to complete the required semiannual narrative questions, which provide a final summary of your program's activities and outcomes.
1.2 Grant Activity Assessment
You must report whether "grant activity" occurred during the reporting period. Grant activity is defined as any instance of obligating, expending, or drawing down grant funds to implement the objectives outlined in your BJA-approved application. Once you report grant activity, your program will be designated as "Operational" for the remainder of the grant's life.
If no grant activity occurred, you must select from the following acceptable reasons:
• In procurement
• Project or budget not approved by agency, county, city, or state governing agency
• Seeking subcontractors (Request for Proposal stage only)
• Waiting to hire project manager, additional staff, or coordinating staff
• Paying for the program using prior federal funds
• Administrative hold (e.g., court case pending)
• Still seeking budget approval from BJA
• Waiting for partners or collaborators to complete agreements
• Other (requires explanation)
This initial administrative data sets the stage for reporting on the specific characteristics and resources of your funded program.
2.0 Defining Program Scope and Resources
This section outlines the core components of your RSAT program's design and resource allocation. Accurately defining these foundational elements—including the services you provide, the staff you employ, and the funds you expend—is crucial for contextualizing all subsequent performance data and providing a complete picture of your program's operational capacity.
2.1 Documenting Service Types
You must report all distinct service types your program provides. The primary categories are:
• Jail-based treatment services: A jail is defined as any confinement facility of a federal, state, or local law enforcement agency whose primary use is to hold persons pending adjudication of criminal charges, persons committed to confinement after adjudication of criminal charges for sentences of 1 year or less, or persons adjudicated guilty who are awaiting transfer to a correctional facility.
• Prison-based services: A prison is defined as any institution under federal or state jurisdiction whose primary use is for the confinement of individuals convicted of a serious crime, usually in excess of 1 year in length, or a felony.
• Juvenile-based services
• Aftercare services
2.2 Quantifying Staffing
Accurate staffing metrics are essential for understanding program capacity and resource allocation. You must report two distinct figures.
Total Treatment Staff Report the cumulative count of all treatment staff who work directly with RSAT participants since the beginning of the grant, regardless of their funding source. For reporting purposes, "treatment staff" includes all treatment employees, such as program managers, case managers, and clinicians. Do not include mentors, volunteers, or interns in this count.
BJA-Funded Treatment Staff Of the total number reported above, specify the number of staff paid for, at least partially, using BJA program funds (including matching funds).

3.0 Performance Measurement for In-Facility Programs (Jail/Prison/Juvenile)
The metrics in this section are designed to track the complete participant journey through the residential portion of the RSAT program. This includes staff readiness and training, participant intake and assessment, the specific services delivered, successful and unsuccessful program completions, and key post-release outcomes. This granular data allows BJA to identify national trends, validate effective treatment modalities, and justify the continued allocation of federal resources to the RSAT initiative.
3.1 Staff Development and Cross-Training
A well-trained workforce is the cornerstone of an effective treatment program. Reporting on cross-training demonstrates a commitment to an integrated treatment and security environment, which is a key goal of the RSAT program.
• For Treatment Staff: You must report whether BJA funds (including match) were used to pay for cross-training during the reporting period. You must also provide a cumulative count of all treatment staff trained since the program's beginning. Qualifying training includes instruction on assessment instruments, motivational interviewing (MI) techniques, accountability, addiction-related topics, and security protocols.
• For Custody Staff: Similarly, you must report if BJA funds were used for cross-training custody staff during the period. A cumulative count of uniformed officers trained since the program's start is also required. This training can include the implementation of assessment instruments, MI techniques, accountability, addiction-related topics, and other security sessions.
3.2 Participant Intake and Assessment
This subsection focuses on tracking who enters the program and how their needs are assessed.
The Proportional Reporting Rule For participant-level data, grantees must report on a number of individuals that is equal to the proportion of the federal funds (plus matching funds) in their total annual budget for the RSAT program. For example, if the BJA RSAT grant and match accounts for 65% of the total annual budget, you must report on 65% of your program participants. If BJA funds pay for a specific number of treatment beds, you should report on the individuals occupying those beds.
Participant Enrollment (Q10)
• Total participants enrolled: The cumulative total number of participants enrolled in the program as of the last day of the reporting period.
• New participants admitted: The number of new participants admitted during the current reporting period.
Risk Assessment and Treatment Planning (Q11-13)
• Participants administered a risk and needs assessment: The cumulative number of participants who have received an assessment designed to identify factors that may lead to reoffending.
• Participants with high criminogenic risks/needs: The cumulative number of assessed participants identified as having high risks and needs.
• Participants with an individualized treatment plan: The cumulative number of participants who have a tailored treatment plan. Note: This number should not exceed the number of new participants admitted during the period (as reported above).
You must also report whether the results of a risk assessment screening tool are used in making admission decisions and provide the specific name(s) of the assessment instrument(s) used.
3.3 Service Delivery and Treatment Components
This section documents the specific interventions and services provided to participants. You must report the number of participants who received each of the following service types during the reporting period:
• Substance abuse and treatment services
• Cognitive and behavioral services (e.g., addressing criminal thinking)
• Employment services
• Housing services
• Mental health services
• Reentry services
• Educational services
• Life skills services (e.g., parenting, anger management, financial preparation)
• Transportation assistance services
• Other services (requires explanation)
Transitional Planning Services You must report the cumulative number of participants who have received transitional planning services. These services are defined as working with clients to develop individualized post-release plans that address needs in housing, employment, and ongoing therapy. This includes enrollment in Medicaid or other insurance programs and prerelease engagement with community correctional agencies. Note: Blanket referrals to community self-help programs do not constitute transitional planning services.
Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) Reporting on MAT is a critical component of assessing modern treatment approaches.
1. MAT Utilization: First, select which option best describes your program's use of MAT:
    ◦ We do not provide MAT
    ◦ We do not have access to MAT
    ◦ Naltrexone (Vivitrol®, Depot naltrexone)
    ◦ Buprenorphine or Buprenorphine/Naloxone (Bup/NX) (Suboxone®,)
    ◦ Methadone
2. MAT Metrics: Second, for the current reporting period, you must report:
    ◦ The number of participants deemed eligible for MAT.
    ◦ The number of participants who received at least one MAT treatment.
3.4 Program Completion and Transitional Outcomes
Tracking program completion and the subsequent transition to the community is vital for assessing program effectiveness. The following metrics apply to the current reporting period:

• Of those completers, the number released to the community.
• Of those released, the number placed under correctional supervision.
• Of those released, the number referred to an aftercare program.
• The number of successful completers with confirmed continuity of care arrangements or reentry plans.

3.5 Substance Use Monitoring and Compliance
As a condition of the grant, programs must conduct reliable drug and alcohol testing. These four metrics are designed to provide a complete picture of substance use at key program junctures: baseline (pre-admission), during treatment, and immediately post-completion. You must report on the following:
1. The number of participants who were tested before admission into the RSAT program.
2. The total (unduplicated) number of participants tested for alcohol or illegal substances during the reporting period.
3. Of those tested during the period, the number of participants who tested positive.
4. The number of participants tested within 30 days after successful completion (while still under supervision) and, of that group, the number who tested positive.

This section must be completed at the close of the grant to provide a final summary of participant outcomes.
First, you must report the final status of all participants served since the start of the award:
• Still participating in the RSAT program
• Successfully completed the program and were released into the community
• Did not complete the program and were released into the community
• Were released into a mandated aftercare program
Second, you must report on reincarceration outcomes for participants released into the community, using the table below.

These metrics for in-facility programs provide a comprehensive view, leading next to the distinct set of measures for aftercare services.
4.0 Performance Measurement for Aftercare Programs
This section details the performance measures specifically for the aftercare component of the RSAT program. While many metrics parallel the in-facility section, they are distinct and are designed to track the participant's progress and outcomes upon their return to the community. Grantees providing aftercare services must adhere to the same "proportional reporting rule" described in Section 3.2.
4.1 Participant Intake and Assessment (Q28-30)
For participants entering aftercare, you are required to report the following cumulative metrics:
• Number of aftercare participants administered a risk and needs assessment.
• Number of aftercare participants with high criminogenic risks/needs.
• Number of aftercare participants with an individualized treatment plan. Note: The number of participants with a treatment plan should not exceed the number of new participants admitted during the period (as reported in Section 4.2).
You must also report whether the risk assessment tool is used for admission decisions into the aftercare program and provide the name of the instrument(s) used.
4.2 Participant Enrollment (Q31)
Tracking enrollment in aftercare is key to understanding the transition from residential treatment. You must report on the following:
• Total number of aftercare participants enrolled as of the last day of the reporting period (cumulative).
• Number of NEW aftercare participants admitted during the reporting period.
• Of those newly admitted, the number with a continuity of care arrangement or reentry/transitional plan.
4.3 Service Delivery and Treatment Components
Similar to the in-facility program, you must report the number of aftercare participants who received each of the following service types during the reporting period:
• Substance abuse and treatment services
• Cognitive and behavioral services
• Employment services
• Housing services
• Mental health services
• Reentry services
• Educational services
• Life skills services
• Transportation assistance services
• Other services (requires explanation)
Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) The reporting requirements for MAT in aftercare mirror those for in-facility programs.
1. MAT Utilization: Indicate if you provide MAT, do not have access, or which medications are utilized (Naltrexone, Buprenorphine, Methadone).
2. MAT Metrics: Report the number of participants deemed eligible for MAT and the number who actually received it during the reporting period.
4.4 Program Completion and Outcomes
For the current reporting period, you must report the total number of participants who successfully completed all requirements of the aftercare portion of your RSAT program.

4.5 Substance Use Monitoring and Compliance
Drug and alcohol testing is also a required component of aftercare. These four metrics are designed to provide a complete picture of substance use at key program junctures: baseline (pre-admission), during treatment, and immediately post-completion. The four key metrics are:
1. The number of participants tested before admission into the aftercare program.
2. The total (unduplicated) number of participants tested during the reporting period.
3. Of those tested during the period, the number of participants who tested positive.
4. The number of participants tested within 30 days after successfully completing the aftercare program and, of that group, the number who tested positive.

5.0 Data Sourcing and Integrity for Aftercare Reporting
Documenting the source of data on criminal justice outcomes is critically important for ensuring reliability and transparency in reporting. This section outlines the required methodology for reporting how your program obtained information on arrests, convictions, revocations, and reincarcerations for aftercare participants.
For each of the four outcome types (arrests, convictions, revocations, and reincarcerations), you must select the one option that best describes your data collection method:
• The grantee is a criminal justice agency (e.g., DOC, probation, parole) and has direct access to this information.
• The grantee has a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU), Memorandum of Agreement (MOA), or linkage agreement with a correctional agency that provides access to this information.
• The grantee conducts public record jail/prison searches online.
• The grantee entered unofficial data (obtained through word of mouth or from participant self-report).
• The grantee does not have access to official justice information.
• Other (requires a specific explanation).
Properly sourcing this data ensures the integrity of your outcomes, which transitions us from quantitative data collection to the qualitative narrative that provides context.
6.0 Semiannual Narrative Reporting Requirements
The semiannual narrative report is a vital complement to your quantitative data. It provides qualitative context, allows you to explain performance successes and challenges, and fosters a direct dialogue with the BJA. These questions must be answered every January and July.
1. What were your accomplishments during the reporting period?
• BJA expects a summary of key achievements, milestones reached, or positive developments that occurred over the last six months.
2. What goals were accomplished, as they relate to your grant application?
• This question asks you to connect your recent accomplishments directly back to the specific goals and objectives stated in your original, approved grant application.
3. What problems/barriers did you encounter?
• Describe any challenges, obstacles, or setbacks you faced during the reporting period that may have prevented you from reaching your goals or milestones.
4. Is there any assistance that BJA can provide?
• This is an opportunity to request specific support from BJA to address the barriers identified in the previous question. You must answer "Yes" or "No." If you answer "No," please explain.
5. Are you on track to fiscally and programmatically complete your program?
• Provide an honest assessment of whether you are on schedule to meet all programmatic goals and expend all funds within the grant's period of performance. You must answer "Yes" or "No." If you answer "No," please provide a detailed explanation.
6. What major activities are planned for the next 6 months?
• Outline your primary goals, activities, and strategic priorities for the upcoming semiannual reporting period.
7. Are there any innovative programs/accomplishments that you would like to share with BJA?
• This is a space to highlight any unique approaches, promising practices, or notable successes that could be of interest to BJA and the wider criminal justice field.

Conclusion: The Role of Performance Data in Program Excellence
Rigorous and consistent adherence to these performance measurement standards is not merely a compliance exercise. It is a foundational practice for achieving programmatic excellence. By meticulously tracking data on program operations, participant progress, and long-term outcomes, you equip your organization to make informed decisions, demonstrate the value of the RSAT initiative to stakeholders, and, most importantly, improve outcomes for the individuals you serve. Your diligent reporting contributes to a national body of knowledge that strengthens substance abuse treatment efforts across the country.
