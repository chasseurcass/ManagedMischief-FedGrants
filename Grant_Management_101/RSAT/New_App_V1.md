# RSAT MAT Quick Start Guide for New Applicants
A step-by-step guide for jails and correctional facilities seeking RSAT funding to launch a Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) program focused on buprenorphine.

---

## 1. Understand What RSAT Will Fund
RSAT funds **substance use disorder (SUD) treatment** delivered in secure settings.  
MAT is allowable when it includes:
- Screening for OUD
- Diagnosis and clinical assessment
- Buprenorphine induction and maintenance
- Counseling that specifically addresses SUD
- Release planning and warm handoffs

RSAT **will not** fund general mental health counseling, FFT, or unrelated services unless tied to SUD treatment.

---

## 2. Decide Your MAT Model
Choose the medications and workflow you will implement.

Most applicants use:
- **Buprenorphine–naloxone (preferred)**
- New inductions **and/or** continuation of community MOUD

Avoid methadone unless contracted with an OTP.

Define clearly:
- Who screens
- Who diagnoses
- Who prescribes
- How induction works
- How patients are monitored
- How you manage release and community linkage

---

## 3. Estimate Your MAT Population
Use last year’s booking and medical data to estimate:
- How many individuals present with OUD
- How many are likely to accept/continue MAT
- How long they stay
- Your average monthly MAT census

These estimates drive your medication budget and staffing needs.

---

## 4. Build Staffing and Training Capacity
You will need:
- A qualified prescriber (MD, DO, APRN, or PA)
- Nursing support for induction and monitoring
- A counselor who can deliver SUD-specific programming
- A case manager for release planning
- Security staff trained on diversion prevention

Train all staff involved before launching.

---

## 5. Design Secure Medication Procedures
Plan for:
- Procurement (local pharmacy, contract, or 340B partner)
- Secure storage and logging
- Directly observed dosing
- Diversion control (mouth checks, documentation, incident reports)
- Daily inventory reconciliation

---

## 6. Estimate Your Budget
Typical expense categories:
- Personnel (prescriber, nursing, counseling, case management)
- Medication costs (estimate $100–$300 per patient per month for buprenorphine)
- Labs and drug testing
- Training and implementation costs
- Storage/security materials

RSAT can fund startup and early implementation, but sustainability planning is required.

---

## 7. Prepare for Release Continuity
Your program must show:
- Partnerships with community MAT providers
- Appointment scheduling before release
- Plan for medication or prescription at release
- Process for warm handoffs

Better continuity = stronger application.

---

## 8. Complete the RSAT MAT Application Forms
The following forms must be submitted:
1. MAT Population & Volume Estimation Form  
2. MAT Clinical Model Description Form  
3. MAT Budget Worksheet  
4. Pharmacy & Diversion Control Form  
5. Staffing & Training Plan  
6. Release & Continuity-of-Care Plan  

Complete all sections clearly and justify assumptions.

---

## 9. Submit Supporting Attachments (If Applicable)
Recommended attachments:
- Draft MAT protocols
- Draft induction procedure
- Staff training plan
- Letters of collaboration with community providers

---

## 10. Contact the Grant Office for Technical Assistance
If you are unsure about:
- Medication estimates  
- Staffing levels  
- Budget assumptions  
- Compliance requirements  

Reach out early. Assistance is available throughout the application window.

# 1. Intake Screening Protocol

## Purpose
To identify individuals with possible Opioid Use Disorder (OUD) upon entry into the facility.

## Responsible Staff
- Intake nurse or medical screener
- On-call prescriber (for review)

## Procedure
1. Conduct intake medical screening within 4 hours of booking.
2. Ask the following standardized OUD questions:
   - “Have you taken opioids (prescription or illicit) regularly in the last 12 months?”
   - “Have you ever been treated with Suboxone, Subutex, or methadone?”
   - “Have you experienced withdrawal symptoms such as nausea, sweating, tremors, or anxiety after stopping opioids?”
   - “Have you overdosed in the past 12 months?”
3. Review jail medical records and prescription monitoring data if available.
4. Check for visible signs of withdrawal (yawning, rhinorrhea, sweating, agitation).
5. Complete the OUD Screening Form and assign one of the following statuses:
   - OUD suspected  
   - OUD not indicated  
   - Continuation of existing MOUD required  
6. Flag “OUD suspected” or “MOUD continuation” for prescriber review.

---

# 2. OUD Diagnostic Confirmation Protocol

## Purpose
To formally confirm or rule out OUD diagnosis in accordance with DSM-5 criteria.

## Responsible Staff
- Licensed prescriber (MD/DO/APRN/PA)
- Behavioral health clinician (optional assist)

## Procedure
1. Review the OUD Screening Form.
2. Conduct a brief structured interview covering:
   - Loss of control over opioid use  
   - Cravings  
   - Continued use despite consequences  
   - Withdrawal and tolerance history  
3. Determine whether the individual meets DSM-5 criteria for mild, moderate, or severe OUD.
4. Document diagnosis in the MAT Clinical Assessment Note.
5. Determine treatment eligibility:
   - Eligible for buprenorphine induction  
   - Eligible for continuation only  
   - Not eligible (provide reason)  
6. Notify nursing and pharmacy of diagnosis and plan.

---

# 3. Buprenorphine Induction Protocol

## Purpose
To safely initiate buprenorphine treatment for individuals with confirmed OUD.

## Responsible Staff
- Prescriber
- Nursing staff

## Required Tools
- Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS)
- Buprenorphine–naloxone 2 mg and 8 mg doses

## Procedure
1. Verify:
   - Confirmed OUD diagnosis  
   - No contraindications  
   - Patient consent  
2. Assess withdrawal using COWS.
3. Initiation criteria:
   - COWS score ≥ 8 (mild withdrawal)  
   - Last opioid use ≥ 12 hours for short-acting opioids  
4. First dose administration:
   - Start with 2–4 mg buprenorphine–naloxone.
5. Reassess after 45–60 minutes.
6. If withdrawal persists (COWS ↑ or unchanged):
   - Administer an additional 2–4 mg.
7. Maximum induction day 1 dose:
   - 8–12 mg unless clinically justified.
8. Document:
   - COWS scores  
   - Time and dose of each administration  
   - Patient response  
9. Provide patient education on:
   - Purpose of buprenorphine  
   - Expected benefits and side effects  
   - Diversion policies  

---

# 4. Buprenorphine Maintenance & Monitoring Protocol

## Purpose
To stabilize and maintain individuals on an appropriate buprenorphine dose during incarceration.

## Responsible Staff
- Prescriber
- Nursing
- Behavioral health clinician

## Procedure
1. Standard maintenance dosing:
   - 8–16 mg/day buprenorphine–naloxone.
2. Daily directly observed administration (DOT).
3. Weekly clinical check-ins during the first month, then every 2–4 weeks.
4. Monitor and document:
   - Withdrawal symptoms  
   - Cravings  
   - Side effects  
   - Behavioral stability  
5. Dose adjustments:
   - Increase in 2–4 mg increments if withdrawal/cravings persist  
   - Decrease for sedation or side effects  
6. Urine drug screens:
   - Baseline, then monthly
7. Counseling integration:
   - At least weekly SUD-focused sessions addressing triggers, relapse planning, and coping skills.
8. Flag for reassessment if:
   - Missed doses  
   - Suspected diversion  
   - Worsening symptoms  

---

# 5. Diversion Control Protocol

## Purpose
To prevent, detect, and respond to buprenorphine diversion.

## Responsible Staff
- Nursing
- Security
- Prescriber (for clinical review)

## Procedure
1. Administer all doses via Directly Observed Therapy (DOT).
2. Require:
   - Visual inspection of mouth pre-dose  
   - Film/tab placement under tongue  
   - Post-dose mouth check after dissolving  
3. Document completion of dose and any irregularities.
4. If diversion suspected:
   - File diversion incident report  
   - Increase monitoring  
   - Notify prescriber  
5. Conduct weekly medication reconciliation:
   - Inventory comparison with administration logs  
   - Discrepancies escalated to medical and security supervisors  

---

# 6. Release & Continuity-of-Care Protocol

## Purpose
To ensure uninterrupted MAT and linkage to community treatment on release.

## Responsible Staff
- Case manager
- Prescriber
- Nursing

## Procedure
1. Identify release dates as early as possible.
2. Schedule community MAT appointment at least 7 days before release.
3. Prepare:
   - 7–14 day buprenorphine prescription OR  
   - A limited supply if permitted by facility policy  
4. Provide release packet including:
   - Appointment details  
   - Provider contact information  
   - Summary of treatment during incarceration  
5. Conduct a final counseling session focused on relapse prevention.
6. Document warm handoff attempt or completion.

---

# 7. Incident Reporting Protocol

## Purpose
To standardize the response to clinical or security incidents related to MAT.

## Responsible Staff
- All staff interacting with MAT participants

## Incidents Requiring Reporting
- Diversion or suspected diversion  
- Missed doses  
- Adverse reactions  
- Behavioral health crises  
- Contraband involvement  
- Medication errors  

## Procedure
1. Complete Incident Reporting Form within 1 hour.
2. Notify:
   - Nursing supervisor  
   - Prescriber  
   - Security supervisor  
3. Document:  
   - Who, what, when, where  
   - Clinical or security impact  
   - Immediate response  
4. Prescriber reviews for clinical follow-up.
5. Security reviews for policy changes if needed.

---

# 8. Program Documentation Standards

## Required Documentation
- OUD screening results  
- Diagnostic confirmation  
- Induction notes with COWS  
- Dose changes and rationale  
- Counseling notes tied to SUD domains  
- UDS results  
- Diversion incidents  
- Release planning notes  

## Chart Format
- All entries must be dated and signed  
- Objective, behavioral language only  
- No assumptions or informal language  

## Audit Readiness
- Treatment plans updated every 30 days  
- Counseling notes aligned to SUD-specific goals  
- Dose change notes reflect clinical reasoning  
- Medication logs reconcile with inventory
