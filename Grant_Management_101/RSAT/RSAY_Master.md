# 3. FAQ / FACT SHEET

## Juvenile Substance Use Disorder Treatment

### (For Providers, Subgrantees, Contractors, and Administrators)

---

### Q1. Do clinicians need to be LADCs to provide juvenile SUD treatment?

**No.**
Any licensed mental health clinician (LPC, LMFT, LCSW, LBP, Psychologist) can provide juvenile SUD treatment IF they use SUD-specific interventions and document them.

---

### Q2. Can general counseling count as SUD treatment?

**No.**
General therapy only counts if it addresses **substance-use behavior**, not just emotions or life stress.

---

### Q3. What makes an intervention “SUD-specific”?

It must directly target:

* high-risk situations
* triggers
* behavior chains
* peer use
* family monitoring
* relapse planning
* pro-use thinking
* skills to not use

If the content could apply to a youth with **no** substance use, it is NOT SUD treatment.

---

### Q4. What documentation is required?

Progress notes must include:

* which SUD domains were addressed
* skills used to prevent or replace use
* relapse planning
* discussion of actual substance-use patterns
* family involvement for supervision/monitoring

---

### Q5. Can a program say “we use FFT” or “we use CBT” and be compliant?

**Only if:**

1. They apply the model to substance use specifically
2. Goals include SUD behavior change
3. Documentation shows SUD-targeted interventions

Otherwise, the model is being used **generically**, not for SUD.

---

### Q6. Does juvenile SUD treatment require MAT?

**No.**
Juvenile SUD treatment is behavioral + family-based, not medication-based.

---

### Q7. How do we know if a youth is progressing?

Programs must track:

* changes in use
* reduction in triggers
* increased skills
* UAs (if used)
* improved supervision
* fewer relapse events

---

### Q8. Is family involvement required?

**Yes**, unless contraindicated.
Family supervision and rule-setting are core components of juvenile SUD treatment.

---

### Q9. What leads to non-compliance findings?

Common failures:

* no SUD-specific documentation
* only general counseling sessions
* no relapse work
* no behavioral chain analysis
* no family monitoring
* unclear goals
* no evidence of skills training

# SECTION 1. PRIMARY SUBSTANCE & BASELINE USE

### Purpose

Establishes the youth’s starting point so progress can be measured accurately.

### Interpretation

* **Daily/near-daily use** → Youth needs intensive SUD focus; reduction is meaningful even if not abstinent.
* **Weekly/Episodic use** → Focus on triggers, peer influence, MH symptoms, boredom.
* **Single or isolated use** → Treatment focuses more on preventing escalation.

### Compliance Red Flags

* No primary substance documented
* Baseline use not captured
* Intake missing frequency/patterns

============================================================

# SECTION 2. MOST RECENT USE DATE

### Meaning

Shows recency and treatment impact.

### Interpretation

* **No use in 30+ days** = Strong progress
* **Use within last 7–30 days** = Mixed progress; evaluate triggers
* **Use within last 7 days** = Relapse or early-stage difficulty

### Compliance Red Flags

* “Unknown” without documentation
* No progress notes explaining recent use

============================================================

# SECTION 3. CHANGE IN USE FREQUENCY

Formula comparing baseline to current pattern.

### Interpretation

* **Improved** = Fewer use events OR longer time between use
* **No change** = Stable but not improving; requires adjustment of intervention
* **Worse** = Increased frequency, new substances, or increased intensity

### Compliance Red Flags

* Marked “improved” without supporting UAs or notes
* Marked “worse” but treatment not adjusted

============================================================

# SECTION 4. UA RESULTS SUMMARY

% of negative UAs over the reporting period.

### Interpretation

* **75–100 percent negative** = Consistent progress
* **50–74 percent negative** = Inconsistent; evaluate relapse plan
* **<50 percent negative** = High concern; revisit assessment

### Compliance Red Flags

* No UAs for youth with known use
* UAs performed but not documented
* Positive UAs with no treatment response documented

============================================================

# SECTION 5. TREATMENT PHASE

Early / Middle / Late / Aftercare.

### Interpretation

* **Early Phase**: Expect exploration, ambivalence, continued use
* **Middle Phase**: Expect skill development, improved monitoring
* **Late Phase**: Expect stability, relapse prevention mastery
* **Aftercare**: Expect skills applied in real-world environments

### Compliance Red Flags

* Youth in early phase > 60 days without explanation
* No shift in phase over months

============================================================

# SECTION 6. ENGAGEMENT LEVEL

Reflects participation and effort, not attitude.

### Interpretation

* **High engagement** = attends sessions, participates fully
* **Moderate** = attends but inconsistent effort
* **Low** = skipping sessions, resistant, disengaged

### Compliance Red Flags

* Low engagement with no follow-up actions
* High engagement marked despite documented resistance

============================================================

# SECTION 7. SUD GOALS PROGRESS SCORE (0–3)

Measures movement toward *substance-use specific* goals.

### Interpretation

**3 (Strong)**
Youth is reducing use, applying skills, following plans, gaining insight.

**2 (Moderate)**
Some improvement; inconsistent but noticeable change.

**1 (Minimal)**
Little progress; youth not applying skills or goals unclear.

**0 (None)**
No movement; goals not addressed or poorly defined.

### Compliance Red Flags

* Scores inflated without documentation
* “0” or “1” scores with no treatment plan adjustments

============================================================

# SECTION 8. SKILL ACQUISITION SCORE (0–3)

Measures mastery of refusal skills, coping, emotional regulation, etc.

### Interpretation

**3** = Demonstrates skills during real-world triggers
**2** = Uses skills with reminders
**1** = Understands skills but rarely uses them
**0** = Cannot identify or use skills

### Compliance Red Flags

* Youth marked “3” with ongoing frequent relapse
* Skill deficits not addressed in treatment plans

============================================================

# SECTION 9. FAMILY MONITORING LEVEL

Evaluates whether caregivers are implementing supervision strategies.

### Interpretation

**High**
Consistent supervision, structure, rules, follow-through.

**Moderate**
Some rules, inconsistent follow-through.

**Low**
Little supervision or unclear rules; caregiver overwhelmed/disengaged.

### Compliance Red Flags

* “High” marked with no family sessions
* Low monitoring but no caregiver interventions offered

============================================================

# SECTION 10. PEER RISK LEVEL

Assesses exposure to peers who use or influence use.

### Interpretation

**Low**
Youth has disengaged from using peers.

**Moderate**
Mixed peer environment; some risky peers remain.

**High**
Actively associates with using peers; strong predictor of relapse.

### Compliance Red Flags

* Peer risk marked “low” despite documented peer contact
* High risk with no intervention plan targeting peers

============================================================

# SECTION 11. HIGH-RISK SITUATION MASTERY (0–3)

Measures how well youth handles drug-use opportunities.

### Interpretation

**3** = Consistently avoids/resists
**2** = Sometimes resists
**1** = Typically caves to pressure
**0** = Cannot identify or resist triggers

### Compliance Red Flags

* Youth marked “3” but has recent use
* No skills training documented

============================================================

# SECTION 12. RELAPSE EVENTS (LAST 30 DAYS)

Count actual incidents, not UA positives alone.

### Interpretation

* **0** = Ideal, but expected mostly in late phase
* **1–2** = Expected in early/middle phase
* **3+** = Requires treatment plan revision

### Compliance Red Flags

* Repeated relapses with no change in treatment
* No relapse documentation despite positive UAs

============================================================

# SECTION 13. SAFETY PLAN UPDATED?

Youth must have updated relapse plan as circumstances change.

### Interpretation

**Yes** = Active relapse planning
**No** = Out of practice; not engaged in relapse prevention

### Compliance Red Flags

* Safety plan marked “yes” but not present in file
* High-risk youth with no updated plan

============================================================

# SECTION 14. OVERALL SUD PROGRESS RATING (0–100)

Composite indicator of treatment response.

### Interpretation

**80–100: Strong Progress**

* Reduction in use
* Skill mastery
* Reduced peer risk
* Improved family monitoring

**60–79: Moderate Progress**

* Some improvement but inconsistent
* Addressable barriers present

**40–59: Limited Progress**

* Skills weak, monitoring low, high peer risk
* Relapse likely
* Requires treatment adjustment

**Below 40: Minimal Progress or Decline**

* Worsening use or behavior
* Interventions not working
* Requires reassessment or higher level of care

### Compliance Red Flags

* High score without supporting data
* Low score with no documented response

# SECTION 15. WHAT REVIEWERS SHOULD DO WITH THIS DATA

This guide is not just for reading — it’s for action.

### When Use Is Not Changing

→ Check for fidelity in skills training, MI, and relapse planning.

### When Skill Scores Stay Low

→ Increase structured CBT-SUD interventions.

### When Family Monitoring Is Low

→ Provide caregiver coaching or family sessions.

### When Peer Risk Is High

→ Implement peer mapping, refusal skills, and pro-social alternatives.

### When Documentation Is Weak

→ Require corrective documentation or training.

### When Composite Score Declines

→ Flag case for supervisor review
→ Consider higher-level services
→ Adjust goals and interventions
